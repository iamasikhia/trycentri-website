Listening to small business owners at the Austin Small Business Expo

What owners told us about their biggest pain points, and how they're using AI today.

Before we build anything, we listen. At the Small Business Expo in Austin, we spent the day on the floor talking with owners and exhibitors, microphone in hand, about how their businesses really run.

We asked everyone the same two questions: what takes up too much of your week, and how are you using AI today? The answers came from every corner of the expo: service providers, consultants, retailers and the people who support them.

What we heard

Three conversations captured the themes we heard again and again across the expo floor.

Brad Henkelman Patent attorney & founder, INRI Patent Law

About half of Brad's work is AI-related, and he already uses an AI patent-drafting tool to expand on points, catch errors and draft the less strategic details. Where it falls short is the part that matters most: the claims.

“At times the AI will make legal mistakes or misunderstand the most valuable parts of the application. I think it's primarily a context issue.”

Gary Capels Founder, G4C Enterprises LLC

Gary runs an energy-savings business (LED and HVAC retrofits, solar, demand response and energy audits), working with a network of subject-matter experts. He's exploring AI to streamline his workflows and to find new customers, including data centres looking to cut their energy use.

“Targeting potential clients is going to be the one that actually helps me boost revenue. That's the biggest hurdle I've got to overcome right now.”

Michelle Kimball Founder & president, 3E AI Solutions

Known as “the AI Lady,” Michelle brings big-business efficiencies to small businesses. She starts every client on a secure foundation, deploying on infrastructure that's FedRAMP Moderate certified, and describes her business as people-first. Alongside her work, she provides free AI education in her San Antonio community.

“I'm a people-first AI business. I don't want anyone losing their jobs or being displaced. I just want you to be able to focus on the strategy of growing your business instead of working on Excel spreadsheets all day, because nobody signed up for that.”

The patterns

  • Context is the gap. Owners already see AI save time on routine work. Where it fails is when it doesn't understand the specifics of their business.
  • Growth starts with leads. Asked what they'd wish for, owners didn't ask for a new tool. They asked for more customers.
  • Trust comes first. Security, privacy and keeping people in their jobs came up unprompted. Adoption follows trust.
  • Education is still missing. Many people only know what they've heard about AI. Hands-on, plain-English guidance changes that.
